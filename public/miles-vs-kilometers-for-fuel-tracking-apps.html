Fuel-tracking apps rely on accurate odometer readings to calculate fuel economy and maintain consistent vehicle records. When traveling between countries that use different distance measurements, keeping those records accurate can become more complicated.

I first ran into this problem while driving in Canada. To make it easier to read speed limits and road signs, I switched my vehicle's display from miles to kilometers. It seemed like a simple change until I realized my odometer was now being displayed in kilometers as well.

Why Miles and Kilometers Matter

The United States primarily uses miles, while Canada and Mexico primarily use kilometers.

Many fuel-tracking apps are configured around a single measurement system and will not adapt when crossing a boarder. If your app expects miles but you enter an odometer reading displayed in kilometers, your fuel economy calculations will become inaccurate.

How Big Is the Difference?

One mile equals approximately 1.609 kilometers.

For example:

  • 100 miles = approximately 161 kilometers
  • 500 miles = approximately 805 kilometers
  • 1,000 miles = approximately 1,609 kilometers

That difference is significant enough that entering the wrong unit can dramatically affect fuel economy calculations and long-term tracking records.

A Common Cross-Border Problem

Some drivers leave their vehicle set to kilometers while traveling internationally because it makes navigating local roads easier. The challenge comes later when it's time to record a fuel purchase.

If your fuel-tracking app is still configured for miles, you'll need to convert the odometer reading before entering it into the app.
